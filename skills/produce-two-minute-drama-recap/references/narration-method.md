# High-retention narration method

Read this reference before drafting or revising narration.

## Evidence basis

The method was generalized from two Chinese vertical drama recaps rather than copied from their wording:

- Sample A: 160.8 seconds, 136 shots, about 1.18 seconds per shot.
- Sample B: 330.0 seconds, 224 shots, about 1.47 seconds per shot.
- Combined spoken-text density: about 365 Chinese characters per minute in both samples.
- Explicit turn language: about 4.1–4.4 meaningful turns per minute.

ASR can misrecognize names. Learn narrative function, not surface phrasing.

## Sentence functions

Make every narration sentence perform at least one function:

1. Establish a visible abnormality.
2. Define the minimum relationship rule needed for the next conflict.
3. Describe a boundary-breaking action.
4. Show an observable reaction.
5. State the concrete consequence that changes the next choice.
6. Introduce evidence that invalidates an earlier explanation.
7. Contrast two characters through parallel visible behavior.
8. Close a decision and open a consequence.

Delete sentences that only repeat an emotion, praise the plot, announce that a twist is coming, or restate what the image and previous sentence already proved.

## Hook construction

Use one of two hook forms:

- **Current abnormality:** relationship violation + unexpected reaction + unanswered reason.
- **Future-climax loan:** visible high-stakes action + withheld cause + promise to explain how events reached it.

Do not begin with the drama title, a biography, a generic judgment, or a list of names. Pay back the hook before the final 15 seconds.

## Causal compression

Write each story unit as:

```text
Expectation → boundary-breaking action → observable reaction → consequence → new choice
```

Use `可是 / 谁知 / 结果 / 没想到 / 原来 / 直到 / 就在这时` only when the next clause changes the audience's model. A turn word without new information is a writing defect.

## Escalation ladder

Do not repeat equivalent humiliations. Move upward through different cost levels:

1. Private ambiguity or a broken promise.
2. Symbolic possession or relationship boundary violation.
3. Physical harm, family pressure, or livelihood risk.
4. Public allegiance, public humiliation, or identity exposure.
5. Evidence that makes denial impossible.
6. An irreversible choice and its social consequence.

The next unit must make the previous excuse weaker or raise the cost of continuing the old choice.

## Character references

Use stable roles such as `主角`, `妻子`, `未婚妻`, `白月光`, `小师妹`, or `母亲` until a proper name is essential. Avoid switching between a name, nickname, title, and pronoun in the same unit.

## Contrast

Use contrast after both sides have visual evidence:

- `一边…一边…`
- `她只顾…却没看见…`
- `从前…如今…`

Prefer one strong contrast over multiple emotional adjectives.

## Dialogue admission test

Keep original dialogue only when it is stronger than narration in at least one way:

- It proves a promise or lie in the character's own voice.
- It is a clear denial that later evidence overturns.
- It publicly declares allegiance.
- It performs an irreversible choice.

Reject logistics, repeated accusations, greetings, and exposition that narration can compress. Preserve complete utterances and reaction air.

## Two-minute density

- Target 110–130 seconds.
- Use 6–8 story units and at least 3 model-changing turns.
- Use 500–650 narration characters as a drafting range.
- Reserve 18–35 seconds for 2–4 dialogue clips.
- Aim for 330–380 spoken Chinese characters per minute after mixing narration and dialogue.
- Rewrite any 20-second span that changes no goal, evidence, relationship, or consequence.

## Ending

Finish an irreversible visible action first. Then open a consequence question.

Good: the agreement is signed; who discovers it next?

Weak: the protagonist is still wondering whether to sign.

Do not insert platform engagement language inside an unresolved dramatic debt. Add a CTA only after the story payoff, and only when the user requests it.
